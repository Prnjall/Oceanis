## PART 1 — NEW CANDIDATE INCIDENTS

### INCIDENT #1

**Year:** 2025
**Incident name:** 2025 Kerala Oil Spill (MSC ELSA 3 sinking)

**Location:** Arabian Sea, off Thottappally, Alappuzha district
**State / coastal region:** Kerala
**Nearest port/city if relevant:** ~14.6 nautical miles off Thottappally spillway; ~13 NM off the Kerala coast; Kochi is the nearest major port (vessel was en route to Kochi)

**Date:** Listing began 24 May 2025; vessel fully sank 25 May 2025, 07:50 IST

**Incident type:** Vessel sinking (container ship) with confirmed fuel-oil release

**What happened:** The Liberian-flagged container ship MSC ELSA 3, en route from Vizhinjam to Kochi, developed a severe list and capsized after flooding in a cargo hold, sinking on 25 May 2025. It was carrying 640+ containers (13 with hazardous cargo, including calcium carbide) and 451.5 tonnes of marine fuel. An Indian Coast Guard surveillance aircraft confirmed an oil slick at the sinking site, and ICG vessels/aircraft carried out dispersant operations under the National Oil Spill Disaster Contingency Plan.

**Oil released / reported quantity:** 84.44 metric tonnes of diesel (MGO) + 367.1 metric tonnes of Very Low Sulphur Fuel Oil (VLSFO) carried in tanks; a confirmed oil slick was observed and treated, though a precise released-volume figure (as opposed to tank capacity) was not published in the sources reviewed.

**If estimates differ:** No conflicting quantity estimates found — all sources (Coast Guard, KSDMA, news) cite the same 84.44 t diesel / 367.1 t VLSFO figures for tank contents.

**Environmental / coastal impact:** Coastal alert issued for Alappuzha, Kollam, Ernakulam and Thiruvananthapuram; containers washed ashore in multiple districts; Kerala government declared it a state-specific disaster; a judicial commission was appointed to assess long-term ecological impact and fisher compensation.

**LATITUDE:** 9.3125
**LONGITUDE:** 76.1360
*(from Kerala SDMA's published position: 09°18.75′N, 076°08.16′E — the vessel's final sinking position)*

**Coordinate type:** Exact
**Coordinate confidence:** HIGH

**Why this coordinate is valid:** Directly published by the Kerala State Disaster Management Authority (a state government authority) as the vessel's recorded sinking position. This is the most authoritative and precise coordinate available for this incident — more precise than the "~14.6 NM off Thottappally" or "~38 NM SW of Kochi" figures used in news coverage (the latter describes the initial listing point on 24 May, not the sinking/spill point on 25 May).

**PRIMARY OFFICIAL SOURCE:** Kerala State Disaster Management Authority — "Ship Wreck – MSC ELSA 3" incident page.
**SECONDARY SOURCE:** Wikipedia, "2025 Kerala oil spill"; multiple Indian news outlets (Deccan Herald, The South First, Onmanorama) corroborating date, cargo, and response details.
**SOURCE LINKS:**
- https://sdma.kerala.gov.in/ship-wreck-msc-elsa-3/
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Kerala_oil_spill

**ELIGIBILITY:** YES
**Reason:** Documented actual oil release (not merely suspected), confirmed by Indian Coast Guard aerial observation and treated with dispersants; exact coordinate published by a state government disaster-management authority; fills a real geographic gap (Kerala coast) with a legitimate, well-sourced incident rather than a forced filler.

### INCIDENT #2

**Year:** 2005
**Incident name:** Mumbai High North (BHN) Platform Fire

**Location:** Mumbai High North offshore complex, Arabian Sea
**State / coastal region:** Maharashtra offshore / Arabian Sea (same broad field as your existing "1993 Bombay High" entry, but a distinct, separately documented incident)
**Nearest port/city if relevant:** ~100–170 km off Mumbai

**Date:** 27 July 2005

**Incident type:** Vessel collision → gas riser fire → confirmed oil spill during firefighting/collapse

**What happened:** The multi-purpose support vessel Samudra Suraksha lost control in rough seas during a medical evacuation and collided with the Mumbai High North (MHN) platform, rupturing a gas riser and causing a fire that destroyed the entire four-platform complex within two hours. 22 people died or went missing. Beyond the fire itself, a clean-up operation was undertaken after an oil spill resulted from the incident.

**Oil released / reported quantity:** One specialist offshore-incident source (Oil Rig Disasters) states a "10 nautical mile oil spill resulted from the fire," requiring a clean-up operation; a specific tonnage was not found in the sources reviewed.

**If estimates differ:** No official crude-volume figure located; only the "10 NM slick" description is corroborated. Flagging this as an evidence gap rather than fabricating a tonnage.

**Environmental / coastal impact:** Clean-up operation conducted after the spill; broader field-level environmental review followed given ONGC's total loss of the MHN complex (per multiple industry sources).

**LATITUDE:** 19.76306
**LONGITUDE:** 71.07806

**Coordinate type:** Exact (platform location)
**Coordinate confidence:** HIGH

**Why this coordinate is valid:** This is the specific, sourced location of the Mumbai High North platform itself (19°45′47″N 71°04′41″E), where the collision, fire, and resulting spill occurred — not a generic "Bombay High" field-center approximation.

**PRIMARY OFFICIAL SOURCE:** FABIG (Fire and Blast Information Group) industrial-accident case file, "Industrial Accident at Mumbai High North, Mumbai, India, 27 July 2005."
**SECONDARY SOURCE:** Oil & Gas Journal (contemporary trade-press report); Wikipedia "2005 Mumbai High fire."
**SOURCE LINKS:**
- https://www.fabig.com/industrial-accidents/mumbai-high-north-india/
- https://www.ogj.com/drilling-production/article/17244183/indias-mumbai-high-platform-lost-in-collision-fire
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Mumbai_High_fire

**ELIGIBILITY:** YES, with a caveat
**Reason:** Strongly documented, exact coordinate, confirmed spill (not merely a fire) — meets all criteria on the evidence side. The caveat is geographic: it sits in the same broad Bombay High field as your existing 1993 entry, so it doesn't diversify the map's coverage the way the Kerala incident does. Include it only if you're comfortable having two separate Bombay High–area entries on the timeline (1993 and 2005); it should not be used as a "gap filler" for a different region.
